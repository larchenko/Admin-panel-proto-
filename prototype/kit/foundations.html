Brand colour

#200766

Carries --primary, the focus ring, the active nav item and the selected-row tint. Greys stay neutral (chroma 0) so this is the only colour competing for attention on a screen full of data. It does not reach the status colours — see below.

What a white-label swap replaces

Brand — replaced

  • --primary, --primary-foreground
  • --accent, --accent-foreground
  • --ring
  • --sidebar-primary, --sidebar-accent, --sidebar-ring

System — left alone

  • --background, --foreground, --card, --popover
  • --secondary, --muted
  • --border, --input
  • --success, --warning, --info, --destructive

Status colours are never derived from, tinted with, or replaced by the brand. A status describes the state of the system, not who owns the product — if green turns violet because a client's brand is violet, the colour stops carrying information and every badge has to be read word by word. If a client's brand lands on a status hue, the brand moves in the UI; the status does not. That is also why --info is a plain blue at hue 240 rather than a lighter cut of the brand: it was the one status that had no value of its own.

Colour

Tokens come in pairs: a surface and the text that sits on it (-foreground). Components use the pair, never a raw colour. Values are OKLCH: lightness 0–1, chroma, hue.

Surfaces & text

Lines & focus

A line is not text: what matters is how it reads against the page behind it, so these are measured against --background. WCAG asks 3:1 for a line that is the only thing marking a control, so --input was darkened until it passes. --border keeps the light shadcn grey — see the note below.

The two greys are deliberately different. --input is the boundary of a control — input, textarea, select, checkbox, radio, outline button — and a user has to see where to click, so it is oklch(0.668 0 0), the lightest grey that still clears 3:1. --border only separates content that is already visible — card edges, table rules, dividers — so it stays at the light shadcn grey and the page does not turn into a grid.

Typography

Archivo. Base size is 14px (text-sm), the shadcn convention for dense admin screens. Three weights only: 400, 500, 600.

Every step of the ladder changes at least two of size, weight and colour. Weight on its own does not carry a level: Archivo 500 against 400 at 14px is a difference you can measure and cannot see. So the three heading levels are all 600 and each one is also a size apart, and 500 is left for emphasis inside a line of body text, where the words around it are the reference.

Three heading levels, and the steps get smaller on the way down: 36 → 24 is 1.5×, 24 → 18 is 1.33×, 18 → 14 is 1.29×, 14 → 12 is 1.17×. That is the shape a scale should have — the further from the body text, the bigger the jump. The page title has to be found from anywhere on the screen; the card title only has to win against the line underneath it.

h2 Dialog / section title Edit instrument 24 / 600 · .section-title .dialog-title
h3 Card / subsection title General details 18 / 600 · .card-title .form-section-title

The overline is the one place where weight carries a level on its own, and it can: against 14 / 400 body it is also two steps smaller and a grey lighter. It is not set in uppercase — that was tried and dropped, see decision 77. Keep those labels to one to three words; the role is scanned, not read. Where the levels sit next to each other: a form label stays 14 / 500 above its input, because the field border already separates the two. A read-only value pairs a 12px muted label with a 14px foreground value — two axes again, no weight needed. Column headers take the overline, so the header band differs from the data by size, weight and colour, not only by its tint.

Spacing

Tailwind's 4px grid. One unit = 0.25rem = 4px. The grid offers every whole number; this product uses seven steps — 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6 — and each one has a job. A step with no job is not a spare value to reach for later; it is a value that will end up meaning two different things on two different screens.

Space is the main grouping device these screens have. The form is not a stack of cards, the table has no wrapper and no radius (decision 50), the sidebar has no rules in it (decision 76), and the page header is closed by space rather than by a line (decision 60). What tells a reader that a label belongs to the field under it, and that the field belongs to the section around it, is that every gap is larger than the gaps inside it. So the ladder below is a set of statements, not a set of sizes.

0.5 2px
Two lines that are one value — a price over its source, a name over its code. .cell .stat-value
1 4px
Items in a list that is one object: nav items, tabs, menu rows, a read-only label over its value. Also the offset of a menu or select from its trigger (decision 80). .sidebar-menu .tabs-list .prop
1.5 6px
Half step, two jobs only: an icon next to 12px text, and the vertical padding of a menu or select row. .field-error .metric-label .menu-item
2 8px
Two things that belong together but are not the same thing: a label and its control, an icon and its label in a button, two buttons in one action group. Also a table cell's vertical padding. .field .btn .page-actions
3 12px
Inside a control — the side padding of an input, a select, a tab, a small button — and the gutter between table columns, which is also what the row bleeds outwards (decision 55). Controls standing in a toolbar take the same 12. .input .table td .table-toolbar
4 16px
Between fields in a form section, and between a block and its own furniture — the toolbar over the table, the count under it. Also the vertical padding of a dialog's header and footer. .form-section-body .table-toolbar .dialog-header
6 24px
The page's own inset, the space between blocks on it, the space between form sections, the gap between two fields side by side, and the inner padding of a card or a dialog. .shell-body .card .form-section .field-row

Not on the ladder: 5 (20px) and 7 (28px) — each sits between two steps that already have a meaning, so it can only blur one of them. Half steps below 2 exist inside controls for optical balance (px-2.5 on an icon button, px-1.5 on a tab count); those are part of a control's drawing, not of the page's spacing.

Four rules

1 · Every gap is larger than the gaps inside it

A form holds together on this alone: 8 between a label and its control, 16 between one field and the next, 24 between one section and the next. Break the order anywhere and the label starts belonging to the field above it. This is also why a group's own inner gap decides the gap around it: a block of read-only values is 16 apart inside, so the space between that block and the one under it has to be 24, not 16.

2 · Across is wider than down

Two fields side by side are 24 apart, two fields stacked are 16. Not a balance judgement: stacked, a label stands between the two controls and does the separating. On one line nothing stands between them, and two bordered boxes 16 apart read as one wide control that has been split in half.

3 · Padding has three tiers

A control is padded 12 (input, select, tab, small button). A band is padded 16 (dialog header and footer, alert, metric cell). A container is padded 24 (card, dialog body, page). Reading inwards, the inset gets smaller, so the frame is always the calmer of any two edges.

4 · 24 is the ceiling on a screen

Nothing on a product screen is separated by more than 24px. This is a dense admin panel; past 24 the eye stops reading a gap as a gap and starts looking for what is missing. Anything that needs more separation than that gets a rule, a surface or a container instead. The single exception is an empty table body (48), where the emptiness is the message. The kit's own pages use 32 and 64 between their sections — they are long-form documents, not screens, and that is not a product rule.

The vertical rhythm of a screen

Measured down the instruments list. Blocks of the page are 24 apart; the table and the two rows that serve it are bound at 16, which is how the toolbar reads as part of the table rather than as a band of its own.

Radius

One base token --radius ( = 4px), the rest is derived with shadcn's steps: base−4 / base−2 / base / base+4, so 0 / 2 / 4 / 8px. Change the base and every corner follows.

Spartan mapping

Token names and the OKLCH format match Spartan UI's theme file one to one. A developer copies the :root block from theme.css into the Angular project's global styles and the theme is done. Icons map to @ng-icons/remixicon; the icon name stays the same (ri-search-line → remixSearchLine).
